Treatment Considerations

Author

Sequoia Fellenbaum

A note about A numbers: to keep track of animals, SCCAS assigns an animal (A) number (or ID) to each animal. In the interest of protecting the animals, owners, and the shelter, these have been truncated to include only the last three digits of the number. There is a small chance that there are duplicates in this explanation because the last three digits are not always unique, but this shouldn’t impact the ability to get the idea across.

Treatment

Each animal’s medical record includes the date that an animal received treatment. However, treatment date is not an isolated variable because the date of collection, time between treatments (if consecutive treatment is required), and time between treatment and collection impact the reliability of the analysis of treatment efficacy.

These considerations leave room for interpretation so the specific guidelines and considerations have been outlined below accompanied by examples to further illustrate the implementation.

Same day treatment

A treatment on the same day as collection should not be considered a treatment as it does not allow the treatment adequate time.

The following case, as an example, was considered to have had zero Nemex treatments.

The animal’s nemex dose number in the final dataset says zero.

Time between treatment

Ideally, treatment is two weeks apart to successfully remove all roundworms. Click here to learn more about the roundworm lifecyle and how it dictates deworming procedures.

Only treatments that were less than 28 days apart were considered. For example, if an animal was treated on 5/29/2025 and 8/5/2025 with sample collection on 9/3/2025, they were considered to be treated only once because the first treatment was over 28 days apart from the second treatment.

Additionally, if treatment occurred less than two weeks before collection date, the animal would still be shedding toxocara and would therefore likely test positive. To adjust for this, treatments administered less than fourteen days prior to collection date were excluded from the treatment count.

Treatment prior to collection date

Then, the treatment prior to collection date was considered. If an animal was treated with nemex 14 days within collection date, this treatment was ignored.

After adjustment:

The case 413 had the second nemex dose more than 14 days before the collection date, so it was considered to have gotten nemex twice. However, the case 907 had the second nemex dose less than 14 days from the collection date, so the nemex dose number was adjusted from two to one.

Roundworm Lifecycle

Toxocara cati is the species of roundworm specific to felines. When de-worming medication is ingested, the adult worms are killed and cleared from the intestine. Unfortantely, these medications only target adult roundworms, leaving behind larvae. This is why repeat treatment is necessary to ensure complete removal of roundworms from the felines intestinal tract. ( Learn more )
